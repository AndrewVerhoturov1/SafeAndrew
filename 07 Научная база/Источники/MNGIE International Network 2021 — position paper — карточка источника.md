## Classic MNGIE

Autosomal recessive TYMP disease with thymidine phosphorylase deficiency causing accumulation of thymidine/deoxyuridine and mtDNA instability.

Typical spectrum:

- severe progressive GI dysmotility;
- cachexia;
- peripheral neuropathy;
- ptosis/ophthalmoplegia;
- diffuse leukoencephalopathy;
- onset often second decade.

## Confirmatory diagnosis

Key confirmation routes include:

- biallelic TYMP pathogenic variants;
- very low thymidine phosphorylase activity;
- elevated plasma thymidine/deoxyuridine.

Urine nucleosides are not equivalent to validated plasma testing.

## Why source matters

MNGIE is one of the rare disorders that can mechanistically unify severe GI dysmotility and peripheral neuropathy, and can mimic CIDP or primary GI disease.

## Treatment caveat

Position paper discusses specialized biochemical-restorative/transplant approaches, but evidence is rare-disease observational and treatment carries major risk.

SafeAndrew must never turn this into self-treatment or routine transplant recommendation.
