## 1. Phenotype first

Before sequencing define:

- EDX phenotype: sensory neuronopathy / axonal sensorimotor / demyelinating-like / mixed;
- onset age and tempo;
- ataxia/vestibular/cerebellar clues;
- GI levels involved: esophagus, stomach, small bowel, colon;
- CIPO objective evidence;
- bladder/urinary smooth-muscle phenotype;
- ptosis/ophthalmoplegia/cachexia/leukoencephalopathy/other mitochondrial clues;
- full-thickness pathology if available.

## 2. Broad first-line molecular layer

For a multisystem phenotype, a narrow single-gene test usually has low efficiency.

Strategy should include broad inherited-neuropathy/neurogastrointestinal genes with explicit coverage of:

- SNV/indels;
- CNV;
- structural variants where available;
- nuclear mitochondrial genes;
- relevant mtDNA analysis where phenotype indicates.

Panel vs exome vs genome depends on laboratory capability; **method coverage matters more than test name alone**.

## 3. Explicit add-on technologies

Some mechanisms need separate/explicit assays:

- RFC1 repeat expansion;
- other repeat expansions according to phenotype;
- biochemical TYMP/MNGIE confirmation;
- mtDNA depletion/deletions in selected mitochondrial contexts;
- difficult genes/regions depending on lab platform.

## 4. Do not force one diagnosis

Possible outcomes:

- one gene unifies neuropathy + GI disease;
- GI genetic diagnosis but neuropathy has second cause;
- neuropathy genetic diagnosis but GI disease independent;
- blended/dual diagnosis;
- unresolved after first-line testing.

## 5. Negative family history

Does not close genetic branch because of recessive disease, de novo variants, variable penetrance and small family structure.

## 6. Escalation after negative first-line

If phenotype remains strong, review:

- exact assay gaps;
- CNV/SV coverage;
- repeat-expansion coverage;
- mitochondrial coverage;
- reanalysis of exome/genome as gene knowledge evolves;
- pathology-guided retargeting.

## 7. Output future agent must give

Not `genetics negative/positive`, but:

1. test technology;
2. gene/variant;
3. inheritance;
4. classification;
5. phenotype fit;
6. what test classes were **not** covered;
7. whether finding explains GI, neuropathy, or both.
