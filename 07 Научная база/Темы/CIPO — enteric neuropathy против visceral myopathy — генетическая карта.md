## 1. First split: mechanism

CIPO can be driven by:

- enteric neuropathy;
- visceral smooth-muscle myopathy;
- ICC/mesenchymal disorder;
- mitochondrial/systemic disease;
- mixed process.

## 2. Visceral-myopathy clues

Increase interest in ACTG2/smooth-muscle genes:

- bladder/megacystis/urinary dysmotility;
- pathology of smooth muscle;
- neonatal/childhood visceral smooth-muscle phenotype;
- family/de novo pattern;
- persistent nonmechanical obstruction.

Genes in published smooth-muscle literature include ACTG2, MYH11, FLNA, MYLK, RAD21, MYL9, LMOD1.

## 3. Neuropathy/systemic clues

Increase interest in mitochondrial/enteric-neural branch:

- peripheral neuropathy/ataxia;
- ophthalmoplegia/ptosis;
- systemic autonomic findings;
- mitochondrial signs;
- pathology of enteric ganglia/nerves.

## 4. Two-diagnosis warning

A visceral-myopathy gene may explain GI disease without explaining peripheral neuropathy.

Likewise an inherited neuropathy gene may not explain CIPO.

Genetic interpretation must explicitly evaluate **unification vs dual diagnosis**.

## 5. Surgery does not settle mechanism

Adhesions/stoma complications can coexist with primary dysmotility.

Etiologic genetics addresses persistent nonmechanical motor disease, not every postoperative obstruction.
